## Full description

Balikbayan Protocol is a cyberpunk console for jumping through your own timeline — and the Philippines' — one year at a time.

Lock in your date of birth and the Protocol opens a time-jump control bounded to your own life: you can ride the stream back to the day you were born, and not one day further. Pick any date in between, engage the jump, and the archive pulls up a full dossier for that year.

Every jump shows you:
— the politics and history that were unfolding in the Philippines
— what was happening in showbiz and pop culture
— the technology and innovations of the moment
— the single most common way ordinary Filipinos talked to each other back then, from letters and telegrams to party-line landlines to pagers, SMS, and smartphones
— the street-level trend or slang that defined that era

The archive is a curated historical compilation spanning the 1940s through today — martial law and EDSA, the texting-capital-of-the-world 90s, Friendster, the smartphone era, the pandemic, and the news still breaking this year. Jump somewhere the archive doesn't reach and the app tells you honestly: the signal's not there.

Your life is also mapped out as a timeline you can tap through directly, a running counter tracks how many days you've jacked in (with a one-tap reset), and a strict one-hour uplink timer forces a hard log-off before you can jump again — because even time travelers need to touch grass.

No account, no ads, no tracking. Everything you enter stays on your device.

Balikbayan Protocol is an independent hobby project. It is not affiliated with any government agency, broadcaster, or news organization referenced in its historical content.
